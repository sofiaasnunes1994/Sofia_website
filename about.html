About the Project

What If Robots Could Help Us Write A New Narrative Of Aging And Culture?

Our goal is not to replace human relationships.
We want to explore how technology can bring people closer together.
We also hope to encourage more positive ways of thinking about aging and cultural diversity.

But, first what is a Social Robot?

A social robot is a machine designed to communicate and interact with people in ways that support everyday activities and well-being.

Societies are becoming increasingly multicultural and technology is becoming more present in our lives. So, we need to question whose experiences and values are represented in its development.

Our Vision

H·age starts from a simple idea: all older adults deserve a seat at the table when designing technologies that may affect their lives.

Did you know that much of today's technology is designed without the people it is meant to serve? Social robots are a particularly good example of this. Most research has involved people from Western, Educated, Industrialized, Rich, and Democratic (WEIRD) countries.

But what if a robot could actually help an older immigrant communicate in their language, navigate a new country, or stay connected to their cultural roots? Sometimes, something as simple as sharing a joke in their mother tongue can make a difference!

Our Research Journey

From imagining a robot to interacting with one over 6 weeks

Study 1 | Listening and co-designing

(2025–2026)

What would a social robot look like if older immigrants helped design it?

We began by inviting 12 older immigrants living in Montreal to participate in three arts-based co-design workshops. Through storytelling, drawing, and robot interactions, participants explored how social robots might fit into their daily lives.

Participants recognized the potential for cultural continuity and well-being but also highlighted the importance of privacy, autonomy, and respect for cultural identities. Their contributions resulted in 35 user requirements for a culturally responsive social robot.

Study 2 | From co-design to experience

(2026–2028)

Can a culturally responsive social robotic intervention be feasible, potentially improve mental health and create meaningful experiences in community settings?

The next phase involves 20 older immigrants in a six-week community-based social robotic program.

Participants will explore group and individual activities with the social robot, including conversations, music, storytelling, and cognitive games.

Most importantly, participants will continue to inform how the technology should evolve.

Local Ethical Advisory Council

The Local Ethical Advisory Council brings together older immigrants with lived experiences relevant to the research. Members help ensure that the project respects participants' cultural backgrounds, values, and needs. They provide feedback on research activities, communication materials, and ethical considerations, helping make the research more inclusive, accessible, and meaningful to the communities involved.

Thesis Advisory Committee

The Thesis Advisory Committee brings together researchers from different disciplines who provide scientific guidance throughout the PhD project. Members offer feedback on the research design, methods, and findings, help address challenges, and support the project's academic development and progress.
